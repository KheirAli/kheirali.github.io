Hi, I'm Al.

Welcome to my personal website. This is where I keep notes on my research, a list of my papers, and a few things I find interesting.

I'm a Ph.D. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Georgia Tech, advised by Sara Fridovich-Keil in the VOILA Lab. I work on diffusion models for inverse problems. The question I keep coming back to is what happens when the prior and the data disagree: how to detect that disagreement from partial measurements, localize it in space, and restore the signal anyway.

My first paper on this, KLIP, appeared at CVPR 2026 and was also my M.S. thesis. Since then I've put out two preprints: SORD, on removing structured corruption with diffusion models, and LACToS, on superpixel-level anomaly detection with Jihoon Hong and Rohan Sanda.

Before inverse problems I spent time on LLM-augmented symbolic reinforcement learning at the SENTINEL Lab, co-developed EmoRationale for SemEval 2025 with the GT-NLP group, and did my undergraduate research on graph-signal denoising at Sharif University of Technology, where I finished my B.S. in Electrical Engineering ranked 6th of 176.

I grew up in Iran and moved to Atlanta for graduate school. I've TA'd six courses, from circuit analysis to computational imaging, and I'm on the boards of the Iranian Student Association at Georgia Tech and the Sharif alumni chapter in Atlanta.

If you want to talk about diffusion priors, OOD detection, or anything in computational imaging, my inbox is open: akheirandish3@gatech.edu.

June 1, 2026

KLIP: localizing distribution shift from measurements

Diffusion models are strong priors for inverse problems, but a strong prior is also a confident one. When the object being imaged contains something the prior has never seen, a diffusion sampler will happily hallucinate something plausible in its place.

KLIP turns that failure into a signal. We compute a spatial KL divergence between the clean diffusion prior and the measurement-conditioned posterior. Where the measurements agree with the prior, the two distributions coincide and the divergence is near zero. Where they contradict each other, the divergence is large. The result is a per-pixel map of distribution shift that needs no calibration set, no labeled anomalies, and no model of the corruption.

What I like about it

  • It is a byproduct of reconstruction, not a separate detector.
  • It works from partial measurements (deblurring, inpainting, super-resolution), not just clean images.
  • The same metric transfers across faces, medical scans, and X-ray security images.

arXiv · CVPR 2026 open access. With Jihoon Hong and Sara Fridovich-Keil.

2026

SORD: support-guided OOD removal with diffusion

Structured corruption, such as a watermark, an occluding object, or a burst of interference in audio, is hard to remove because you usually need either paired data or a model of the corruption. SORD uses neither.

The observation is that the support of the clean signal is informative even when the corruption is not. We use the available measurements to guide diffusion restoration past the corrupted region, letting the prior fill in what the measurements can't say, and nothing more. It works on images and on audio.

With Sara Fridovich-Keil. arXiv:2610.33194 · code.

October 2026

LACToS: conditional typicality of superpixels

Pixel-level anomaly scores are noisy and image-level scores are blunt. LACToS sits in between: it scores superpixels by their conditional typicality under a diffusion prior, using an efficient statistical proxy so the whole thing runs at practical speed.

We evaluate robustness across medical imaging, faces, and X-ray security screening. Led by Jihoon Hong, with Rohan Sanda and Sara Fridovich-Keil. arXiv.

2023–2025

Earlier work

LLM-augmented symbolic RL (ICASSP 2025)

At the SENTINEL Lab with Duo Xu and Faramarz Fekri, I built a framework that uses a language model to decompose tasks into landmark-based subtasks and to generate first-order logic rule templates, which are then refined with inductive logic programming. The appeal was getting interpretable policies out of a pipeline that still benefits from a big pretrained model.

EmoRationale (SemEval 2025)

With the GT-NLP group I co-developed a retrieval-augmented approach to multilingual emotion detection that produces evidence for its predictions, without task-specific fine-tuning.

Graph empirical mode decomposition (Sharif, 2022–23)

My undergraduate research in the DSP lab was on denoising graph signals with Graph EMD in the low-SNR regime. It was my first real exposure to the question that still drives my work: how much of a signal can you recover when the measurements are bad?

On teaching

I've been a teaching assistant for six courses: Foundations of Computational Imaging and Circuit Analysis (ECE 2040) at Georgia Tech, and Probabilistic Graphical Models, Probability and Statistics, Artificial Intelligence and Biological Computation, and EEG Signal Processing at Sharif.

The thing that carried over from the probability courses to computational imaging is that students rarely get stuck on the math. They get stuck on what the symbols are standing in for. Most of my office hours are spent drawing pictures of distributions.
